## Sourcing and storytelling | Individual · 7 minutes
> Watch Clip C and answer the questions.

[play https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p2hnrfN58H4 03:25 05:13 "Clip C – sourcing and storytelling"]
[transcript "Transcript (after you have finished)"]
Designer: The client really gravitated towards Parisian pieces and artisans, and we really leaned into that process and met with a lot of incredible craftsmen. And I traveled overseas sourcing items such as artwork and furniture, but also looking for those small detail items like bed linen and cutlery and crockery that really elevate the home to another level, and they're pieces that you can't find in Australia necessarily.
Designer: This process provides a sense of rarity and uniqueness, and it often brings a sentimental value or memory or a feeling that's evoked through these pieces as well, which really layers the space.
Rob Mills: I think as a designer, we have a responsibility to tell a singular story that's really strong. How we do that is to come together as a team, as interior designers and architects and landscape architects, and discover what that story is, and then write the story both in words and visually. We use this to set the foundation of the design for the house.
Rob Mills: Each time we design, we learn. We learn from our clients, our collaborators, each other, and it's, honestly, it's as I get older, it's what I value the most. The relationship that is created by the building contributes to how our clients feel when they eventually move in and live in the space. That was the most rewarding part of this journey.

[teacher]
**Video.** The Local Project, “Architect Designs a Home That Disappears Into the Hill (House Tour)”, with Rob Mills of Rob Mills Architects and a designer from his team. Australian accents, slow and clear speech, background music throughout.

**Clips.** A 00:06–01:25: the client, the site and the Murray River. B 01:26–03:24: the holistic approach and a walk through the house (skylight, staircase, study, kitchen, terraces). C 03:25–05:13: sourcing from Parisian artisans, the “singular story” and what Mills values. The intro (00:00–00:05) and credits (05:14–05:26) are skipped.

**Grammar from the script.** Participle clauses: “Walking into the space, your eye is immediately drawn…” (01:47) and “Traveling down the staircase, you experience…” (02:05). Pseudo-clefts and nominal relatives: “How we do that is to come together as a team” (04:14) and “it's what I value the most” (04:47). Point out the dangling participle in the first example: it is the visitor, not the eye, who walks in, which is common in speech but best avoided in a written design statement.

**Spelling.** “Traveling” in the transcript is American spelling; both spellings are accepted in the answers.

**If time runs short.** Cut the Word formation exercise and use only the first two discussion questions.

**Fast finishers / homework.** Write a 120–150-word design statement for one of your own projects that tells a “singular story”. Use at least one participle clause and one sentence beginning with “What…” or “How…”.
