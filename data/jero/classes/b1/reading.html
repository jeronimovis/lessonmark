Part 1 · 5 questions · signs, notices, messages

No. 04

Why was this written?

  1. What is it: a sign, a label, a text message, an ad?
  2. Why does it exist? To warn you, sell you something, tell you off?
  3. Say the message to yourself in your own words.
  4. Find the option that means that, even if it uses different words.
  5. Careful with options that say more than the text does.

Part 2 · 5 questions · matching

No. 05

Three things, one match

  1. Each person wants three things. Underline them.
  2. Look for those things in the texts. They’ll be in other words.
  3. The right text has all three. Not two. Three.
  4. Underline where you found each one, so you can check.
  5. Three texts won’t match anyone. That’s on purpose.

Example

Lucía wants a course that is cheap, at the weekend and outdoors.

1 · cheap

2 · weekend

3 · outdoors

The text with two out of three is there to trick you.

Part 3 · 5 questions · one long text

No. 06

How does the writer feel?

  1. Read the whole text once, quickly, before the questions.
  2. Underline the key words in each question.
  3. Find that part of the text. The questions go in order.
  4. Answer in your head first, then look at A, B, C and D.
  5. Can you say why the other three are wrong? If not, read again.

The questions ask

How did the writer feel?

What does she think about…?

Why did he do this?

The last question is about the whole text, so you need the big picture.

Part 4 · 5 questions · gapped text

No. 07

Spot the links

  1. Read the text with the gaps. What is each paragraph about?
  2. Read the eight sentences.
  3. Look at the sentence before the gap and the one after it.
  4. Same topic? Same tense? Singular or plural?
  5. Read it all again at the end. Three sentences are left over.

Part 5 · 6 questions · choose the word

No. 08

Which word fits?

  1. Read the title and the whole text first.
  2. Look at the word after the gap. It often decides the answer.
  3. Try all four words in the gap. Say them in your head.
  4. Pick the one that means the right thing and sounds right.
  5. Read the text again with your answers in it.

Part 6 · 6 questions · write one word

No. 09

One word. Usually a small one.

  1. Read the whole text first. Don’t fill anything in yet.
  2. For each gap, ask: what kind of word is missing?
  3. Read the words on both sides of the gap.
  4. Write one word, and spell it correctly. It matters here.
  5. Read your text. Does every sentence make sense now?
